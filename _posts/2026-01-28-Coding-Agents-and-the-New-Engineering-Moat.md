title: Coding Agents and the New Engineering Moat
---

Software engineering is shifting from writing every line of code to orchestrating systems that can write, refactor, and operate code for us. Coding agents are getting good enough that the bottleneck is no longer typing, but deciding what to build, how to validate it, and how to keep it resilient in production.

This does not mean software engineering disappears. It means the role fragments into new levels of leverage.

### The new capability ladder

At the base level, PMs and designers will be able to build basic systems with agent help. The scope will be small but meaningful: internal tools, landing pages, workflow automations, or small apps with low operational risk.

Senior engineers will use agents to build more complex systems quickly. The productivity jump comes from being able to spec, delegate, and review rather than manually building every subsystem. Speed increases, but so does responsibility for correctness and system behavior.

Principal engineers will focus on optimizers and infra-level systems. This is where reliability, cost, and scale live, and where agentic coding still needs strong human guidance, especially in performance, security, and architecture tradeoffs.

### Where the moat moves

The technical moat is less about raw coding speed and more about the ability to:

- Use a variety of agents and tools effectively to build and scale systems faster.
- Solve ambiguous problems with crisp constraints and measurable outcomes.
- Lead businesses, not just builds: sales, negotiation, product-market fit, and fast iteration loops that reveal what is not working.

In other words, the moat is becoming a mix of systems thinking and business leadership, not just syntax and frameworks.

### Skills that grow in value

As coding becomes more automated, several skills become more durable:

- AI transition skills: helping teams move from manual workflows to agentic ones.
- Data engineering: building pipelines that keep models, analytics, and products fed with reliable data.
- Infrastructure engineering: reliability, security, and cost optimization remain hard problems.
- Research or applied experimentation: knowing how to test uncertain ideas quickly.

### New business opportunities

The shift opens a wave of new businesses and service models.

**Custom software deployments**
This looks like initial builds plus agentic retainers: self-healing code, server-side agents that update packages, and automated fixes when builds fail. This is viable because serverless and elastic infrastructure costs keep trending downward, making continuous automation affordable.

**Workflow automation consultancies**
Companies will need help redesigning their processes for agentic workflows. Expect AI transition consultancies that implement and maintain these systems, tuned to specific teams and industries.

**Tailored agentic workflows, then productization**
Early on, most agentic workflows will be custom. Over time, patterns will emerge: the same playbooks, the same data flows, the same approval gates. Those patterns will be packaged into new products that offer turn-key automation with minimal setup.

### The core change

Software engineering is not going away. It is getting redistributed. The highest leverage moves toward defining the right problems, shaping systems that can adapt, and connecting engineering decisions to business outcomes. Coding agents are the accelerant. The moat is how you use them, how you reason through ambiguity, and how you keep the system valuable in the real world.
